We leave Dublin on **5 October 2026** and fly home from Tokyo on the **23rd**, looping back the long way via Doha. Everyone keeps calling it "the Japan trip," but it is really a **Korea + Japan trip**: we land in Seoul, wander east and south, cross over to Japan, and end in Tokyo for the flight home. Rachel and I have been arguing happily over maps for weeks, so I am turning the planning into a small series here on the blog.

This first post is the part I found hardest: **deciding where to actually stop.** Two countries, seventeen hotel nights, and a shortlist that kept ballooning. So I did what any over-caffeinated engineer would do — I built a *menu*. Not an itinerary, a menu: 34 candidate destinations, each weighed by what it adds, how many nights it deserves, whether it is better as a day trip, and how badly the crowds and logistics might bite.

> [!NOTE] How to read this
> This is a **selection tool**, not a fixed plan. Nothing is booked. Prices, journey times, opening hours, and crowd levels are **planning estimates** checked in **early August 2026**, not quotes or live forecasts. Once we tick the must-dos, this menu becomes a dated route in a later post.

## How much actually fits

The hard limit is time, not enthusiasm.

- We have **17 hotel nights**, from a probable Seoul arrival on **2026-10-06** through the night of **2026-10-22**.
- Keep at least **4 nights in Seoul**. The first is really an arrival/recovery night, which leaves only three full days.
- Keep at least **5 nights in Tokyo** at the end, because the flight out of Narita is fixed for the afternoon of **2026-10-23**.
- That leaves about **8 nights** for everything else. Four extra bases would make the trip transfer-heavy; **two or three** is realistic.
- A two-night stay usually buys just **one full sightseeing day**. A one-night stay only earns its place for a special evening, an onsen/ryokan, or somewhere directly on the route.
- Each hotel change quietly eats **half a day** once packing, checkout, stations, luggage, and check-in are counted.

> [!TIP] My birthday math
> My birthday lands on **2026-10-08**, so we are keeping that day gentle and in Seoul. Small thing, but it anchors the front of the trip.

## Crowds, hype, and where the tourists actually go

This is the part most "top 10" lists skip, and it is exactly what changes a plan. Here is my **crowd-pressure scale** for October 2026:

**1/5** quiet · **2/5** light tourism · **3/5** busy at recognizable sights · **4/5** very touristy with queues or packed transport at peak times · **5/5** crowding can dominate the day or break the plan.

A split rating like **"5/5 core, 3/5 elsewhere"** matters — Tokyo, Seoul, Osaka, and Kyoto are not uniformly crowded. These are **planning judgments**, not official scores; they blend national travel surveys, official visitor counts and congestion warnings, the October calendar, and whether demand spreads out or compresses into one street, bus, ropeway, or attraction. The "hype check" asks an honest question: *does this place still earn scarce trip time if you remove the famous photo, the perfect weather, or the headline ticket?*

### Dates that bend the whole trip

- **2026-10-09 to 11:** Korea's **Hangeul Day** long weekend overlaps Suwon's closing festival weekend and major autumn travel. Seoul palaces, Suwon, Gyeongju, Jeonju, Seoraksan, and intercity trains are all more exposed.
- **2026-10-10 and 17:** Andong's scheduled fire events concentrate visitors into limited regional transport; the 17th is also a weekend.
- **Through 2026-10-18:** Jinju's lantern festival is an evening crowd event, with the final weekend likely stronger than a normal night.
- **2026-10-10 to 12:** Japan's **Sports Day** weekend raises domestic leisure traffic and accommodation pressure. Avoid a first-time regional rail sprint or a famous onsen in this window if the Korea→Japan crossing can move.
- **2026-10-16 to 18:** Nikko's 16–17 Oct Toshogu festival, autumn foliage, and a weekend all collide. This is the **worst Nikko window** of the trip — central Nikko only, or an overnight.
- **2026-10-19 to 22:** weekdays improve museums and city sights, though they don't erase Kyoto icon crowding or Nikko foliage traffic. Best days for ticketed Tokyo experiences and a carefully timed day trip.

### My hype filter before adding a base

1. Require **two independent reasons** to add a hotel base. One photo, café, ropeway, museum ticket, or night event is normally a day trip or a skip.
2. Re-judge **Uji, Nagoya, Osaka, and Andong without the Nintendo, Ghibli Park, USJ, or fire-event outcome.** If a place then has no value, don't book a hotel until the dependency is secured.
3. Use an overnight where it **transforms the crowd experience** — Koyasan, Takayama, Miyajima, Kawaguchiko, upper Nikko. It adds much less in Suwon, Nara, Kobe, Uji, or Himeji.
4. Don't stack two **5/5-pressure days** back to back. Queues and packed transport remove the fallback time we rely on for meals and comfort.
5. A place can be a genuine local favorite and still be wrong for *this* trip. Beppu, Gangneung, Jeju, Hakone, and Nikko are all real domestic favorites whose route, room, bath, weather, or transport tradeoffs are equally real.

Whatever we pick, we'll keep the **last night or two near a fast Narita link** (Skyliner from Ueno/Nippori, or N'EX from Tokyo/Shinagawa/Shibuya/Shinjuku).

**When splitting into two hotels is actually worth it:** only if we bolt on an **onsen or Fuji night** — Hakone, Kawaguchiko, or upper Nikko — which is a separate one-night base, not a second Tokyo hotel. If we truly wanted two Tokyo vibes, the clean split is **Asakusa/Ueno first** (old-town east + Skytree) then **Shinjuku/west last** (Ghibli, Warner Bros.) — but across 5–7 nights the lost half-day rarely pays off.

## Turning the menu into a route

A few rules keep the plan from turning into a transfer marathon:

1. **Choose the Korea shape first:** Seoul only; Seoul + Busan; Seoul + Gyeongju/Busan; Seoul + Andong; or Seoul + one special nature/garden base.
2. **Choose the Japan entry point:** Fukuoka for a west-to-east route, Osaka/Kansai for a classic Kansai trip, or Nagoya/Tokyo for fewer bases.
3. **Keep one rail direction** and avoid zig-zagging: e.g. Fukuoka → Hiroshima/Okayama → Kansai → Nagoya → Tokyo.
4. **Use day trips to avoid one-night hotels:** Suwon from Seoul; Nara or Uji from Kyoto; Kobe from Osaka; Himeji en route; Kamakura from Tokyo.
5. **Don't combine every specialist stop.** Sokcho, Suncheon, Tongyeong, Damyang, Jeju, Nagasaki, Beppu, Takayama, Matsumoto, Koyasan, Nikko, Hakone, and Kawaguchiko each cost disproportionate transfer time.

> [!NOTE] A gentle note on food and comfort
> We travel with some dietary planning to do and a preference for rooms with a bit of breathing space, so a few rules ride along with every choice: the big cities (Seoul, Osaka, Kyoto, Tokyo) get more nights; every regional overnight needs food arranged in writing plus some room supplies; ropeways, caves, and immersive exhibits always have an open-air or skip-it alternative; and we favor larger rooms with an opening window over capsule hotels and windowless boxes. None of this is a hardship — it just quietly shapes the map.
