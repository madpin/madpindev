I used to think gluten-free bread was just regular bread with the good part removed. Mix, rise fast, bake, slice it warm and wonder why the inside is wet.

The fridge changes that. A slow **12 to 18 hour cold ferment** does not just delay the bake — it lets the dough build **organic acids** that mimic sourdough tang, and it gives **psyllium husk** time to fully hydrate into the gel that holds a gluten-free loaf together. No gluten network means no fast warm rise to hide behind. Time is the structure.

This loaf is built for that overnight wait. You mix for **about 25 minutes**, give it **1 hour** at room temperature, then the fridge does the work while you sleep.

> [!NOTE]
> **The honest headline:** active time is about **25 minutes**. Total time is **13 to 19 hours**, almost all of it hands-off in the fridge. The two grids below split the job so you never hold the whole loaf in your head at once.

## 🛒 Ingredients and Why They Are There

### The Dry Base

| Ingredient | Quantity | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sorghum flour (or brown rice flour) | **150 g** | The main flour body. Mild, slightly sweet, and naturally gluten-free — it gives the crumb its structure without tightness |
| Tapioca starch / flour | **100 g** | The stretch. Tapioca gelatinises into a chewy, elastic crumb that keeps the loaf from being crumbly |
| Potato starch (not potato flour) | **100 g** | The softness. Holds water, lightens the crumb, and slows staling — potato flour would be heavier and gummier |
| Teff flour or buckwheat flour | **50 g** | The depth. Dark, earthy flavour and colour, so the loaf tastes like bread instead of a starch cake |
| Fine sea salt | **10 g** (about **1½ tsp**) | Seasons, tightens the gel, and slows the yeast so the cold ferment stays steady instead of frantic |

### The Binding and Liquid Phase

| Ingredient | Quantity | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole psyllium husks (or **16 g** powdered) | **20 g** | The binder. Forms a pliable gel that traps gas the way gluten would — this is the whole structure, do not skip it |
| Warm water, split in two parts | **450 g** total at **38 °C (100 °F)** | Hydrates the flours and the psyllium. Warm, not hot — hot water kills yeast, cold water slows it |
| Instant dry yeast | **7 g** (1 packet) | Produces **carbon dioxide** for lift and **organic acids** during the long ferment for sourdough-like flavour |
| Honey or maple syrup | **15 g** | A quick meal for the yeast at the start, and a little sugar for crust colour |
| Olive oil or melted butter | **15 g** | Coats the starches for a tender crumb and carries aroma — butter is richer, olive oil is cleaner |
| Rice flour, extra for dusting | **extra, as needed** | Non-sticky dusting for the basket and the work surface — wheat flour would reintroduce gluten |

> [!TIP]
> **Do not add extra flour to the sticky dough.** Gluten-free dough is wetter than wheat dough by design. If you dry it out, the psyllium gel cannot do its job and the crumb turns to cake.

Three things the grids make obvious.

**Only about 25 minutes need you.** The long cells are waiting: **60 minutes** for the first rise, **12–18 hours** in the fridge, **45 minutes** to preheat the Dutch oven, and **2 hours** to cool. Set a named timer at each one and leave.

**Grid 2 starts before you go to bed.** Put the empty Dutch oven in the oven the night before if your oven allows it, or at least remember it in the morning — preheat is the step most people forget when the dough is already cold and ready.

**The cool is part of the recipe.** **Two hours** on a rack is not patience theatre. Gluten-free crumb keeps setting as it cools; slicing warm makes it gummy no matter how good the bake was.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Don't forget:** Dutch oven in the oven **before** you preheat, score **straight from the fridge**, and a **2 hour** cool. Those three lines are the whole bake.

## 🍳 Method

### 1. Make the Psyllium Gel

Whisk **20 g whole psyllium husks** into **350 g warm water at 38 °C**. Set timer: psyllium gel, **5 min**. It should turn into a thick, pliable gel. If you are using **16 g powdered psyllium**, start with less water and whisk fast — powder clumps.

### 2. Activate the Yeast

In a cup, mix the remaining **100 g warm water**, **15 g honey**, and **7 g instant yeast**. Set timer: yeast bloom, **5–10 min**. Done when the surface is foamy. No foam after **10 min**? Make a fresh cup with fresh yeast.

> [!NOTE]
> Visual cues beat the clock here. Water at **38 °C** is warm to a fingertip, not hot. Much above **50 °C** and you start killing the yeast.

### 3. Combine the Dry Flours

In a large bowl, whisk **150 g sorghum flour**, **100 g tapioca starch**, **100 g potato starch**, **50 g teff flour**, and **10 g salt**. Whisking aerates the starches so the gel spreads evenly instead of finding dry pockets.

### 4. Mix the Dough

Add the **psyllium gel**, the **yeast mixture**, and **15 g olive oil** to the dry flours. Mix with a stand mixer on paddle for **4–5 min**, or beat hard by hand with a sturdy spatula. The dough will be wetter and stickier than wheat dough — that is correct. Do not add flour.

### 5. First Rise at Room Temperature

Cover with a damp towel or plastic wrap. Set timer: first rise, **60 min**. Done when the dough has grown about **30–40%**, not doubled. While you wait: rinse bowls, line a banneton or bowl with a tea towel, and dust it with rice flour.

### 6. Shape Into a Boule

Turn the dough out onto a rice-floured surface. Shape gently into a smooth round and place it smooth-side down in the basket. Gluten-free dough tears if you stretch it — press and tuck, do not pull.

### 7. Cold Ferment in the Fridge

Cover the basket tightly with a plastic bag or reusable cover to trap moisture. Set timer: cold ferment, **12–18 hours** at **3–5 °C (38–41 °F)**. A **12 hour** ferment is tangy and open; **18 hours** is deeper and more sour. While you wait: put the empty Dutch oven in the oven.

### 8. Preheat the Dutch Oven

Preheat the oven with the Dutch oven inside to **230 °C (450 °F)** for at least **45 min**. Set timer: preheat, **45 min**. About halfway (**22 min**), check that the oven actually reached temperature — cold ovens are the number one cause of a flat gluten-free loaf.

### 9. Score the Loaf

Pull the dough straight from the fridge — do not let it warm up. Flip it onto parchment, dust the top lightly with rice flour, and score one deep slash about **0.5 inch** deep with a sharp razor blade.

### 10. Steam Bake Covered

Transfer the loaf on its parchment into the hot Dutch oven. Drop **2 ice cubes** under the parchment, outside the loaf margin, to make steam. Lid on immediately. Set timer: covered bake, **35 min** at **230 °C**.

### 11. Bake Uncovered

Remove the lid, lower the heat to **210 °C (410 °F)**, and set timer: uncovered bake, **20–25 min**. Done when the crust is deep golden brown and the base sounds hollow when tapped.

### 12. Cool Completely

Transfer to a wire rack. Set timer: cooling, **2 hours**. Do not slice warm. Gluten-free bread continues setting internally as it cools; cutting warm dough makes the interior gummy.

## 🔬 Why It Works

### Psyllium gel replaces gluten

Gluten is a stretchy network that traps gas. Psyllium husk forms a **hydrocolloid gel** that does the same job with viscosity instead of elasticity. That is why the gel step comes first and why the dough stays sticky — the gel is the structure, not a side ingredient.

### Cold ferment builds flavour the warm rise cannot

Yeast at fridge temperature works slowly, producing **organic acids** in small amounts over many hours. Those acids are what make an authentic sourdough taste complex instead of just yeasty. A fast warm rise gives lift but almost no flavour; the overnight ferment gives both, in that order.

### Steam sets the crust, dry heat finishes it

**Ice cubes** under the parchment flash to steam, which keeps the crust pliable while the loaf expands in the first **35 min**. Removing the lid for the last **20–25 min** lets the crust dry out and brown. Swap those two phases and you get either a pale, soft loaf or a loaf that bursts before it sets.

## 🧯 Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Rescue plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crumb is gummy | Sliced warm, or underbaked inside | Wait the full **2 hours** next time; if already baked, toast slices until the centre firms up — do not re-bake the whole loaf |
| Loaf is flat and dense | Dutch oven was not hot enough, or score was too shallow | Preheat a full **45 min**, score **0.5 inch** deep straight from the fridge, and keep the lid on for the full **35 min** |
| Psyllium gel is lumpy | Powdered psyllium added to water all at once | Whisk the powder into the dry flours first next time, then add water; for this batch, whisk the lumps out hard and keep going — they usually hydrate |
| Crust is pale and soft | Lid removed too early, or oven ran cool | Keep the lid on **35 min**, then bake uncovered at **210 °C** until deep golden — colour is the cue, not just the timer |
| Loaf tastes yeasty, not tangy | Cold ferment was too short | Push to **18 hours** next time; use the extra time for the preheat, not for a shortcut |

> [!WARNING]
> **Food safety:** do not leave the baked loaf at room temperature for more than **2 days**. Slice and freeze it after day one if you will not finish it — gluten-free crumb dries out fast, and freezing protects both quality and safety.

## ✨ Make It Yours

- **Tangy:** use **buckwheat** instead of teff and ferment the full **18 hours**.
- **Neutral:** use **brown rice flour** instead of sorghum and olive oil instead of butter, for a cleaner crumb.
- **Rich:** use melted butter instead of olive oil and add **10 g** extra to the dough.
- **Seeded crust:** brush the scored top with water and press on **15 g** mixed seeds before the covered bake.
- **Small loaf:** halve everything, use a **15 cm** Dutch oven, and cut the covered bake to **25 min**.

## 🧊 Storage and Reheating

Store the whole loaf, cut-side down, in a cloth bag at room temperature for **1 day**, or slice and freeze in a sealed bag for up to **1 month**. Reheat slices in a toaster or a **180 °C** oven for **5 min** until crisp at the edges. Never microwave a slice to "soften" it — it turns the crumb rubbery.

> [!WARNING]
> **Do not refrigerate the baked loaf.** The fridge accelerates staling in gluten-free bread. Freeze it instead, and reheat from frozen.

## 💭 Final Thoughts

The first time I made this, I sliced it after **20 minutes** because I could not wait, and the centre was wet enough to stick to the knife. The same recipe, cooled for **2 hours**, was a completely different loaf. Cold fermenting does not make gluten-free bread behave like wheat bread — it makes it behave like itself, with time doing the work gluten used to do.
